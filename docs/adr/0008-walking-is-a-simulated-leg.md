# Walking is a simulated Leg with real cost

> **Amended 2026-08-11 by session F** ([`plans/0021`](../../plans/0021-trips-legs-and-the-pedestrian-layer.md) → *Session F's brief*). **The decision is untouched** — walking is a simulated Leg, a car commute is never fewer than three, and Buildings connect to the pedestrian network rather than to the Road Graph. What changed is **what a pedestrian network is made of**: this ADR predates the mode mask and asked for *sidewalk edges alongside street edges*, which [`0072`](0072-the-mode-mask-is-saved-on-the-arc-and-the-segments-is-derived.md) rejects by name. The pedestrian network is a **subgraph**, not a second edge set. Four consequences and the revisit trigger are amended in place below, each marked. Three decisions came out from under this one and have records of their own: [`0074`](0074-side-of-street-is-a-property-of-the-access-point-not-of-the-graph.md), [`0075`](0075-a-leg-is-a-plan-and-a-traveller-is-a-cursor.md), [`0076`](0076-the-trip-fate-set-is-closed-at-four-and-a-fate-names-the-journey.md).

**Every Trip is a sequence of Legs, and walking is one of them — simulated, costed, and capable of failing. Buildings connect to a pedestrian network, not directly to the road graph.** A car commute is therefore never one Leg; it is at minimum `walk → drive → walk`. This holds from the first line of code, with no transit implemented and possibly none ever.

## Why

The received framing of this decision is "do we want public transit?" That framing is a trap, and it is the one Citybound fell into. Eickhoff built cars first and planned to add pedestrians and transit later; cars-only silently shaped the lane model, the routing model, the Trip model, and the zoning model, and pedestrians were still unbuilt when the project stopped. The recurring community critique — *"if your only mode of transport is cars you can hardly call what you're building a city"* — was never answered.

**The thing that cannot be retrofitted is not transit. It is walking.**

In a car-only Trip model, a Building *is* a node on the road graph and a Trip is a single Leg. That one assumption propagates much further than it appears to: a Lot's value becomes a pure function of road access, density carries no access cost, and Trip cost is entirely drive time. Undoing it later means rewriting the Trip model, the cost function, the Lot valuation, and every piece of balance tuning built on top of them.

Whereas the moment walking is real, a mundane commute is already multi-modal — mode transitions, transfer points, and a cost function that must trade walking minutes against driving minutes — with zero transit code. Adding a bus later becomes *inserting a Leg type into machinery that already handles Legs*. That is incremental. The jump from one-Leg to many-Leg is not, which is precisely why it never happened in Citybound.

Two things make this unusually cheap for us in particular:

- **Sidewalks essentially never stress.** Under [`0007`](0007-stress-driven-simulation-detail.md), fidelity follows congestion, and pedestrian ways are the least congested thing in the city. Walk Legs will be Statistical ~~approximately~~ **always** — a `distance / speed` lookup. ~~They almost never enter the expensive regime.~~

  > **Amended by session F: *approximately always* was never a hedge, because nothing could ever have promoted a foot Segment.** The design has exactly two promotion triggers. **Stress** is `volume / capacity` over vehicles, and `CONTEXT.md` → Fidelity bars pedestrians from contributing to it — *"a crowded pavement must never promote a Segment."* **Force-promotion** fires on a downstream Microscopic Segment being full, which is vehicular by construction. So the expensive regime this bullet says walk Legs *almost never* enter has **no door into it at all**, and under [`0070`](0070-an-unbuilt-mechanism-is-not-a-design-constraint.md) the mechanism the hedge gestures at is *undesigned* rather than merely unbuilt — it may not constrain the Leg model. `CONTEXT.md` → Fidelity and `03 §3.7` were already categorical (*"a **walk Leg is always Statistical**"*, *"walking has one fidelity, **permanently**"*); this ADR and `adr/0007` were the two hedged copies, and the difference between the readings is a whole mechanism, since the categorical one means **milestone 5b builds no promotion path for a foot Segment and owes no hole for one**. `03 §3.7`'s transit trigger — *a stop is a queue with a capacity* — remains the sole reopener and is unchanged. **This settles the structure, not the claim underneath it**: whether pedestrian networks saturate is [`plans/0002`](../../plans/0002-open-questions.md) §B-16, it is *measurable*, and its machine is 5b. `adr/0007`'s matching wording belongs to session **E** and is routed to [`plans/0012`](../../plans/0012-corpus-audit.md) rather than corrected here.
- **It makes walkability emerge rather than be scored.** Mixed-use, density, and street life are otherwise formula terms bolted onto Lot desirability. Here a corner shop is viable because people can physically reach it on foot, which is `EMERGENCE` and `LEGIBLE CAUSE` doing real work rather than being asserted.

And it creates a failure mode worth having. **Severance** — a neighbourhood cut off by an arterial with no crossing — becomes something the player can accidentally build and then diagnose, rather than something the game has no vocabulary for. The inability to walk is as interesting as the ability.

## Rejected

**Buildings on the road graph, pedestrians as rendering decoration.** Cheaper today, and it is what SimCity does. Rejected because it is the specific irreversible decision this ADR exists to avoid, and because decorative pedestrians violate the standing rule that every visible agent is a promise you have to keep.

**Transit first, walking later.** Incoherent in the other direction — transit *is* walking plus a vehicle Leg. There is no version of a bus trip that does not begin and end on foot.

## Consequences

- **Trip object count roughly triples.** Every car commute is 3+ Legs instead of 1. Legs are small and mostly Statistical, but the Trip table must be sized for this rather than for a Leg-per-Trip assumption.
- ~~**The road network needs a pedestrian layer**: sidewalk edges alongside street edges, and crossing edges at junctions. This is real work, not a free consequence.~~ Arterials deliberately have no sidewalk and no crossings except at authored junction pieces — which is the mechanism that makes severance emerge rather than being a scripted penalty.

  > **Amended by session F: the sidewalk half is a mask bit, the crossing half is real, and the work is already done.** This consequence is two claims joined by an *and*, and they have opposite fates.
  >
  > **Walking adds no Segments.** A Street's footway is *the same Segment with the foot bit set on each Arc that permits it*, so the pedestrian network is a **subgraph** rather than an addition — `CONTEXT.md` → Segment (*"the mode mask is an edge property, not a second edge set"*) and → Road Graph (*"not two parallel networks"*). One graph is what buys one **Epoch** covering both networks, one revalidation path, and a multi-Leg Trip routed by a single mode-aware search rather than stitched across two structures. **A one-way street does not break this**, which is the objection that most often reaches for the second edge set: [`0072`](0072-the-mode-mask-is-saved-on-the-arc-and-the-segments-is-derived.md) puts the mask on the **Arc**, so `modes_forward = Car | Foot` and `modes_backward = Foot` carries cars one way and pedestrians both, within one Segment.
  >
  > **The crossing clause survives literally.** What *is* additional is the small set of **foot-only Segments** — crossings where an Arterial severs a Street, footpaths, pedestrian precincts. `CONTEXT.md` → Segment already names them and says why they matter: *"they are the edges **Severance** turns on, so nothing may size the graph by omitting them."*
  >
  > **And *"real work, not a free consequence"* was true and is now discharged — by milestone 5a, not by 5b.** `RoadGenerator` writes per-direction masks, turns every `foot_crossing_every`-th severed crossing into a `RoadKind.FootPath` carrying `Foot` and not `Car`, and lays foot-only paths off `foot_paths_per_thousand_blocks`. Both halves of this consequence are built. Nobody noticed, because naming them in one breath made the refused half and the built half look like one obligation.

- **What the subgraph reading gives up is *side of street*, not direction.** *(Session F, and it is the one thing this reading actually costs.)* One Segment is one centreline, so there is no separate north and south footway. Direction is handled — that is what the per-Arc mask is for — but *which side you are on* is not in the graph, and it is real: the corner shop **opposite** is not the corner shop **next door**. It does not follow that the graph should carry it. Side of street is a Tile-scale distinction beneath a Segment's block-length resolution; **Severance does not need it**, because an Arterial's Arcs never granted a foot route at all; and pricing it in edges would double the foot graph to buy seconds against a Budget scored in minutes. [`0074`](0074-side-of-street-is-a-property-of-the-access-point-not-of-the-graph.md) keeps the information and puts it on the **Access Point** instead, where it costs one bit.
- **Buildings need a pedestrian access point distinct from a vehicle access point.** These are usually the same place and occasionally are not, and the distinction is what lets parking later become a real location without restructuring anything.
- **The Commute Budget now spans modes.** Walking minutes and driving minutes are the same currency and both count against it. This is a direct application of the standing rule that path cost must be the quantity the player is scored on.

  > **Session F examined this and it ships literally — one currency, clock minutes, no per-mode weight.** The objection is real and F states it rather than ducking it: transport economics values walking time at roughly twice in-vehicle time, so a weight would be more faithful to people. It is nonetheless refused, because **a weight inside the Budget breaks the rule the Budget exists to enforce.** The player is shown a commute in minutes; if the Budget scores *perceived* minutes then the scored quantity is no longer the displayed one, which is SC4's unlearnability failure arriving through the door this consequence locked. **The distaste for walking has a home that is not the cost function**: [`0032`](0032-services-are-delivered-by-trips-not-by-coverage.md) puts mode on a **Provider List entry** — *"how I get to work is decided when the job is taken"* — so a mode preference belongs to the choice model, one layer up. Routed = scored = displayed = minutes. **This ADR therefore gains a consequence it can be held to**, and 5b avoids a hash-bearing number rather than acquiring one.
- **Parking becomes an open question rather than a non-question.** The walk Leg has to start and end *somewhere*. Deferring a real parking model is defensible; pretending the question does not exist is not.

  > **Session F named the placeholder, and found that the obvious one is a trap.** The placeholder is the Building's **vehicle Access Point**: until milestone 7 there is nowhere else a car can be, so a flanking walk Leg runs from the pedestrian Access Point to the vehicle one — which are equal by construction today, making those Legs **zero-length**. The retrofit at milestone 7 is **one endpoint swap**, because a ~~parking Bin~~ **`Car Park`** has an `Address` and so does a Building (`0074`, `0120`), so nothing restructures. That much is cheap.
  >
  > ⚠ **The trap is that zero is inside the range of legitimate answers.** A Building with its own garage genuinely has a ~zero door-to-car walk, so a zero-length parking walk is indistinguishable from a *modelled* one and **the placeholder cannot announce itself** — the failure slice 6's `pool` scope was built to avoid, where a hole degrades into a plausible answer instead of throwing. **And the degradation is rewarding in the wrong direction**: if the vehicle Access Point is ever reachable as a **fallback** from a failed Parking Shed query, a full car park costs *less* than an empty one, because the driver arrives at the door instead of parking three blocks away — so the player is paid for under-building parking, and the payment grows with the shortage.
  >
  > **Two rules follow, and they are written now because F is the last document before the code exists.** *(1)* The vehicle Access Point is milestone 5c's **sole** path to a parking location, never a fallback from anything; at milestone 7 that path is **replaced**, not supplemented. *(2)* `CONTEXT.md` → Parking Shed already states the correct milestone-8 behaviour — *"scarcity **widens** the shed rather than blocking the Trip… the Trip fails only if it exceeds its Commute Budget"* — and this ADR adds the prohibition that makes it enforceable: **an exhausted Shed widens and may never return the vehicle Access Point.**
  >
  > **Direction of error, recorded rather than hidden.** Until milestone 7, driving carries **no access cost at all**, so any balance struck between 5c and 8 is optimistic about cars, and milestone 7 makes every car Trip *worse* rather than better. A pessimistic placeholder distance would invert that, and is refused: it is a hash-bearing number standing in for a scarcity mechanism that does not exist, which is [`0070`](0070-an-unbuilt-mechanism-is-not-a-design-constraint.md)'s forbidden move exactly.
- **Unwalkable geography is now expressible**, which means it is now possible to build a city that fails in a way earlier drafts could not represent. That is the point, but it needs to be legible: a Trip Fate of *no route found* for a destination 50m away must be diagnosable, not mysterious.

## What would trigger revisiting

Only one thing plausibly does: if pedestrian Leg volume turns out to dominate simulation cost at metropolis scale despite being Statistical. ~~The mitigation to reach for first is coarser sidewalk topology — one pedestrian edge per block face rather than per street segment~~ — not deleting the Leg. Removing walking after the fact reintroduces exactly the irreversibility this decision was taken to avoid.

> **Amended by session F: the trigger stands and its mitigation was already spent before this ADR named it.** `CONTEXT.md` → Segment fixes a Segment at *"roughly a block-length link"*, ~30,000 at 1M Citizens — so **one pedestrian edge per block face is what the graph already is**, and there is no finer sidewalk topology to coarsen because, after the amendment above, there is no sidewalk topology at all. Coarsening further would mean a second, coarser foot edge set, which is the two-parallel-networks structure `CONTEXT.md` → Road Graph rejects by name.
>
> **What replaces it: the lever is the search, not the topology.** A walk Leg's cost is the mode-aware search over the foot subgraph; `distance / speed` is free once the route is in hand, and [`0075`](0075-a-leg-is-a-plan-and-a-traveller-is-a-cursor.md) makes a Leg store that cost rather than the path. So the mitigations to reach for, in order, are **route-side**: cache the walk route, or approximate a short walk as straight-line plus a detour factor. Deleting the Leg stays refused for the reason this decision was taken.
>
> **This ADR may not be cited as having a number.** The refuting figure is peak pedestrian density per block face at 1M against the density at which walking speed falls — [`plans/0002`](../../plans/0002-open-questions.md) §B-16, typed *measurable* by session D task 0, machine **milestone 5b**. Under [`0043`](0043-a-claim-a-measurement-could-settle-must-not-be-settled-by-argument.md) no session may close it, and session F did not.
>
> *Why the amendment exists at all: a revisit trigger whose mitigation does not exist is a trigger nobody can act on, which is [`0073`](0073-a-local-workaround-is-not-a-discharge-and-a-finding-about-shared-code-must-reach-it.md)'s failure one layer up.*
