# 0051 — The four pillars, and a city to photograph

Scoped 2026-09-01, against `main` at `3eb0c62`. **The first plan under
[`docs/07`](../docs/07-the-drawing.md), and the first work in this repository whose deliverable is a
picture rather than a mechanism.**

⚠ **Run in a git worktree**, because `ruleset-key-audit` held uncommitted edits to all 30 Rulesets,
`RulesetLoader.cs`, `SchemaDump.cs`, the schema and the golden fixtures at the time this was scoped.
***A new Ruleset authored on top of a moving key surface is a file that will be wrong before it is
read***, and a worktree is what made the two bodies of work separable at all.

---

## What this plan is for

[`docs/07 §9`](../docs/07-the-drawing.md) ranks what a screenshot actually costs and finds that
**most of it is not the asset pipeline**. The top four items are a sun, a camera, a lens and a
night — and none of them commits the project to a pipeline, an art style or a mesh.

**So they are the cheapest possible test of the fifth pillar** ([`docs/07 §5`](../docs/07-the-drawing.md)):
if a city with a low sun, a free camera, a tilt-shift and lit windows is not worth photographing,
then no amount of authored geometry was going to save it, and the question was answered before
anybody spent a week on meshes.

## What this Ruleset must not be used for

🔴 **`pictured.toml` RATIFIES NOTHING, MEASURES NOTHING, AND MUST NEVER BE CITED FOR A NUMBER.**

Every other shipped Ruleset carries a header saying it is *a demonstration rather than a city*, and
each isolates one mechanism so that a figure taken from it names one cause. **This file is the exact
opposite by construction**: it turns everything on at once, so a census taken from it has every
mechanism in the world as a candidate cause and can attribute an outcome to none of them.

⚠ **That is not a defect and it is not a compromise.** A city to photograph is a city with a coast
*and* shops *and* schools *and* traffic *and* ruins in the same frame, because a picture of a place
is a picture of things coexisting. ***What makes it useless as an instrument is exactly what makes
it useful as a subject.***

⚠ **It must therefore never enter the assertion tier as a source of a figure**, and any test that
loads it asserts a **shape** — that it parses, that it builds, that the tables are non-empty — and
never a quantity.

| **F21** | ⚠ **THE NIGHT LIGHT IS THE SAME OCCUPANCY NUMBER THE DAY ALREADY DREW, AND ONLY THE NIGHT ONE IS LEGIBLE.** By day a shut opening and an open one differ by a shade of grey nobody reads at street distance; at night the same share is a black wall against a lamp, and a half-let quarter is visible from a kilometre up. ***No overlay has ever managed that*** — which is `01`'s own argument for overlays arriving from the opposite direction |
| **F22** | ⚠ **A FAR FADE THAT IS RIGHT BY DAY IS WRONG AT NIGHT.** `glazed` carries `NEAR_FADE`/`FAR_FADE`, so a window past 1,100 m contributes nothing — and at night that is exactly the distance at which the lights ARE the picture. A second, hash-free term spreads the same warmth over the wall keyed on occupancy alone; the per-window draw is noise at that range and noise that flickers is worse than no lights at all |
| **F23** | 🔴 **THE WALLS LEAKED LIGHT, AND WHAT LEAKED WAS A WINDOW ONE PIXEL WIDE DRAWN IN THE GAP BETWEEN TWO.** `buildings.gdshader` took its antialiasing footprint from `fwidth(withinX)` — and `withinX` is a **`mod`**, so at every bay boundary it falls from `wide` to zero in a single pixel and `fwidth` reports **the whole bay**. Three and a half metres of blur then went into a smoothstep whose job is to be a centimetre wide, and the pane half-opened along the seam: `inX` comes out at **0.235** on the boundary pixel, with the same thing happening horizontally off `withinY` at every storey line. ⚠ **The report was *light leaking through the walls, as if they were not fully attached*, and the marks are in fact perfectly regular** — a dashed column between every column of windows, dashed exactly where the storeys are. ⚠ **IT WAS INVISIBLE BY DAY FOR THE SAME REASON THE LIT WINDOW EXISTS AT ALL** (F21): a quarter-open pane is a shade of grey on a grey wall and a quarter of a lamp on a black one, so the night made a two-milestone-old albedo defect legible. ✅ **Fixed by taking the footprint off `u` and `up`**, which run the wall's length without wrapping and carry the same derivative everywhere the `mod` does |
| **F24** | ⚠ **A CITY GOES TO BED IN AN ORDER, AND AN ORDER IS THE ONE THING A BRIGHTNESS CANNOT CARRY.** The `night` uniform row 5 shipped is a **darkness** and therefore symmetrical — it says the same thing at nine in the evening and at four in the morning — so every window keyed on it could only be lit for the whole night or none of it, and the city switched on and off twice a Day. ***The missing input is a direction of travel***, not a finer ramp. `night_phase` replaces it outright: 0 at the last of the light, 0.5 at solar midnight, 1 at the first of it, pinned at 1 all day. Each opening then draws **its own two hours** — when it lights and when it goes out — and the sky's only remaining job is to say which night it is. ⚠ **THE PHASE IS LINEAR IN TIME AND NOT IN THE SUN'S HEIGHT**, which was tried first on paper and is wrong: height is a cosine, so it barely moves for the hours either side of midnight and then races at the horizon — a phase built from it puts the whole city to bed in the first twenty minutes. ⚠ **The going-out draw is CUBED and that is the shape of the request rather than a tuning**: flat, the city empties at an even rate all night; cubed, the wall is full at 21:00, **29%** of it is still burning at solar midnight and **3%** an hour and a half before dawn. ⚠ **The last lamp goes out at phase 0.86 against a sunrise at 0.92**, so the city is dark for a moment before the sun comes up — reversing those two makes a dawn read as a light switch |
| **F25** | 🔴 **A LAMP WAS FADING IN, AND IT WAS FADING IN TWICE.** ***Nothing in the world fades on: a switch is thrown and the room is lit.*** The defect was one mistake made in two places and neither was noticed while the other stood. **First, `night` was a multiplier on `EMISSION`** — so it was not only the wrong *shape* of quantity (F24) but the wrong *kind*, arriving at the lamps as a **dimmer** and swelling every window in the city up through dusk and down through dawn. **Second, the per-window switch was a `smoothstep`** twelve thousandths of a night wide, put there against a sparkle that cannot happen: every opening draws its hour off a continuous hash, so no two switch on the same frame to begin with. ✅ **Both gone** — `night` is deleted rather than demoted, and the switch is a `step`. ⚠ **The DISTANT haze still ramps and that is correct**, because it is not a lamp: it is a few thousand of them summed at a kilometre, and a sum moves smoothly however hard each switch is. ⚠ **The lamps now come on in a lit sky** — the first hour is drawn from 0.06, which is about sunset — and that is what dusk looks like rather than a defect |
