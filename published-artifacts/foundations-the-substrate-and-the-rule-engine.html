ACT 2

What planning found

One shape, four times: the tests were green because they agreed with the code

the substrate

Rerolling the world seed produced the same world, shifted by one entity

The normative random draw opened with two externally chosen coordinates added together, so only their sum reached the hash: draw(seed=1000, entity=1) equalled draw(seed=1001, entity=0) bit for bit, at every Tick and for every purpose. Writing the first known-answer vectors is what exposed it. ⚠ It cost nothing — no Input Log, no State Hash baseline and no save existed yet, "which is the entire argument for the instruction that this be the first thing built."

3a · finding 27

The corpus had a worked example that nothing ever executed

Whoever transcribed the simulation model's bakery into the loader's test fixture silently dropped the money line. So the document's own worked example destroyed one money per baking, in contradiction of a settled ADR, for six slices — and the test suite agreed with the code instead of with the document.

3a · finding 34

A task in the plan was invalidated by a decision in the same plan, thirty lines below it

Task 10 asked for a production chain over two or three Goods; a decision further down the same file had settled the scope it needed as a named hole that throws. Neither noticed for seven tasks. "The task list is the one part of a plan nobody re-reads after the plan is being executed — which is precisely when its assumptions are being invalidated one at a time by the section beneath it."

3b · item 1

A board item turned out to be a correction rather than a decision

It was already answered in three places. What was actually wrong was a sentence in the simulation model that argues against itself — it states a transition carries both content hashes and then, in the same breath, says a replay needs content rather than "the news that they changed", and a hash is the news that they changed. ⚠ The third instance in the same section of the same document.

3b · item 5

A refusal cannot be tested while the thing it refuses lives in the binary

The pollution kernel radius was a const and was supposed to be Ruleset data. That blocked the slice, not merely annoyed it: "a designer cannot change it in a file, so a reload cannot detect that they did. The refusal and the defect have to be fixed in the same task or the refusal is untestable theatre."

3c · the naming

The slice was named for growth, and only decline was honestly expressible

Of the seven links in the growth chain: no Unplaced Pool, no price surface, no land value, no construction cost, no private capital, no bid-price contest. The response was not to skip creation — "a slice that only frees rows cannot discharge the obligation it inherited" — but to say what the work actually buys: churn. Rows created and freed on a cadence is "the only thing that tests row recycling, handle generations, stale-handle detection and teardown under repetition. None of that has ever run."

ACT 3

What shipped

Eight Tick phases, a subscription model instead of a poll, and a hot reload two orders of magnitude inside its own precedent

Milestone 3a — the Rule engine, and the number that was right by cancellation

The laboratory benchmark timed one phase over a Tick on which every Building is due, on the reference machine under powersave: 82.84 ns per evaluation, flat to 1.8% across two orders of magnitude, allocating zero at every rung. That licensed a tripwire: Bin Rule evaluation fits the Tick budget while fewer than ~188,000 evaluations occur per Tick.

Then the same cost was measured in a real city — 1M Citizens on minimal.toml, per-Tick slope across two run lengths so startup and JIT fall out — and it came in at ~329 ns per evaluation. Four times worse.

What that did to the budget ledger is worse than making a row bigger

That row read 10.42 ms per Tick, multiplicand guessed. The truth is 6.4 ms at a measured 11,586 due Rules. The multiplicand was ~5× too high and the unit cost ~2.8× too low, so the product landed within 40% of the answer by cancellation — "and a row that is right by cancellation is worse than one that is wrong." The tripwire moved 3.3×, and the simulation model's own bakery example does not fit at 4× under the new number.

Milestone 3b — the Citybound risk, measured

Edit one rate in a copy of the Ruleset from 8 to 2, reload it into a running session at Tick 200 of 400, and the city differs: the hash trace diverges from the reload on, the Unplaced Pool goes from 9 to 101, and demolitions go from 3 to 11. 0.70 s against a pre-built runner — three runs at 0.71, 0.71, 0.69 — and 2.8–3.5 s through dotnet run, which re-checks five projects on the way past.

Citybound's recorded number, which is the evidence the reload decision was written on, is a 60–120 second warm rebuild. ⚠ "The margin is two orders of magnitude, and the honest caveat is that this city is 1,000 Citizens and 400 Ticks."

And the test could not have been run the way the plan asked for

A recorded log names a Ruleset by content hash, so the first edit to the file makes the log name something that no longer exists — and editing the file is the loop being measured. "A recorded session is therefore exactly the wrong instrument for this claim, however carefully it is written. Nobody had noticed this, and the plan's own task description asked for the runner to accept several Rulesets as though that were sufficient."

Milestone 3c — the tripwire held, and it is what hid the real defect

The Zone Rule sweep measured 1.56× across a 1,000× Zone, against a bound of 2× — with both guards holding: the linear control moved 989×, and the denominator drifted 2.3% across the whole sweep. The verdict was that the model's claim is "false in the letter and true in the substance": sampling is constant-cost, and what grows is the memory hierarchy. Zone size is not the variable; working-set size is, and it is bounded by the map.

Then a later measurement found the shipped number was scale-dependent

The sample was an absolute count of Lots per cycle, so what the city feels is inversely proportional to its own size: a Lot is visited once per 0.12 Day at 1,000 Citizens and once per 117 Days at 1,000,000. At target scale the shipped Ruleset built nothing in 2,000 Ticks. And the tripwire is what hid it — "a test that confirms the stated claim while the unstated one fails is worse than no test." The repair replaced the count with a duration, from which the count derives.

ACT 4

What is still open

One invariant with no machinery, one wheel tier with no milestone, and a correction the ledger has not paid

lint 4

Thread-count equivalence still needs machinery that does not exist

Classified as not writable yet, and the reasoning is the point: "a test asserting equivalence across no parallelism passes vacuously and then keeps passing after the parallelism arrives." Everything runs serially, and two documents disagree about which phases may be parallel versus which are — a contradiction the slice named and left standing.

4

The coarse Day wheel has no milestone at all

Milestone 4 built the fine Tick wheel and the second tier was scoped out. Every Day countdown in the design — Life Stages, Need decay, the housed re-evaluation — presumes a tier that nothing states is being built. ⚠ And a caveat left behind with it: the due-Rule counter is per-table, so a second wheel would need a second counter rather than inheriting this one, and nobody has noticed that yet.

⚠ docs

The slice ledger still carries a headline that was withdrawn

It says the city settles five-sixths homeless. That headline was withdrawn: the figure is fixed by knobs the fixture chose on purpose — a Rule drawing on a Resource nothing produces — "and carries no information; it would have read much the same at any occupancy." The number that was evidence is the second one: nearly half the declared places that existed stood empty while 300 people were pooled. That is a missing door, not a balance of rates, and building it took vacancy to 10%.

⚠ docs

Two documents describe milestone 4 as finishing what an earlier slice half-built

The plan's own first finding contradicts it: the fine wheel was not half-built, it was built — both halves of the invariant registered and tested. "The deliverable the gating session named was delivered before the session ran." The correction is filed and not paid.

3a

The in-situ cost figure replaced one unrepresentative number with another, and says so

It was measured on a Ruleset whose own header says it models no city — 2 Rule Instances per Building against a guessed 450 per 1,000 Citizens, which is 3.75. ⚠ "So this does not ratify the 450."

the substrate

The randomness independence property is asserted and not tested

Both implementations of the draw were written by one author in one sitting, so they corroborate each other and nothing else. "Closing the real property needs a reader who has not seen this code."

2

Declaring a column derived allocates it — it does not make anything rebuild it

The allocation-by-declaration trick closes the hash's coverage hole and leaves the rebuild's wide open: a column can be declared derived while the structure deriving it lives outside the world entirely, in which case a load restores the rows and the index is simply never built. Nothing fails, because a column nobody reads yet is a column nobody reads yet. ⚠ Found four milestones later, by the only test that asks.
