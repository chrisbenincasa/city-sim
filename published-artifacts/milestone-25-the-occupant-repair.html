Milestone 25 — the Occupant repair

A bakery holds its own flour, its own bread, its own till and its own oven. The building holds a street address and some floors. Making the build agree with that sentence took one day, five tasks and forty-five findings.

What this page is, and what owns the facts on it

A summary of one closed milestone, assembled from plans/0040, plans/0003 and docs/06. Every fact here is owned somewhere else — plans/0040 owns the findings, the ADRs own the decisions, plans/0003 owns what is done. Where this page disagrees with any of them, they win.

Figures are quoted with the clause that says what they measure rather than the digits alone (plans/0012 Cause 5). ⚠ Like every published artifact, this page is invisible to the mechanical checks in tests/Borough.Tests/Corpus/, which are document-to-document over *.md — so nothing will tell you when it goes stale.

ACT 1

The risk it retired

A Rule Instance named premises, so no money term could resolve to a payer

Milestone 25 arrived carrying one risk and left having retired a different one. That is not drift — the milestone was capped at group A on the day it was decomposed, and the project's rule is that a milestone must name a risk it actually retires, so the cell was rewritten to match what group A does.

The risk as rewritten — retired

That a Rule Instance names premises rather than an actor, so no money term can resolve to a payer.

A Bin's owner stops being a HandleColumn<Building>; a Rule Instance starts naming an Occupant. A local money term now has an actor to resolve to, and a tenant's Bins leave with the tenant.

The risk as it stood — moved to milestone 27

That the economic actor does not exist in the build, so no Rule can spend money.

Untouched by this milestone. World.CreateBusiness had zero src/ callers throughout, and nothing funded one. Group A makes the payer nameable; it does not make one exist.

Why it was small on its main axis

This was a correction, not a design change, and that is what sized it. adr/0113 decided it; adr/0114 wrote the target shape down in as many words — "World.FindBin takes an owner rather than a Building slot" — and the build never arrived. A revisit trigger that has already fired is a decision waiting to be finished.

ACT 2

What planning found

Decomposition made the milestone smaller in one place and found a hole two ADRs had left

Session V (plans/0039) scoped the work and closed into two ADRs. Decomposition then surveyed the tree against them — and the survey corrected session V in two places, confirmed it in four, and found one thing neither ADR answered. That last one is why a plan document exists at all rather than a task list written straight from the records.

F1 · made it smaller

The Rule half is cheaper than it was costed

Session V named six sites reaching a Rule Instance. Every one goes through the derived BuildingRules list, not the saved Building handle — which has five readers and zero test readers. The blast radius is the list; the column is nearly free.

F3 · found a hole

The capacity path has a severable hop and neither ADR answers it

adr/0141 keys capacity on the building kind; adr/0142 makes unpremised a legitimate steady state. So an unpremised Business owns Bins whose capacity is declared by premises it does not have — and the existing code silently resolves that to capacity 0 while the Bin holds stock.

F8 · sized it

The columns are cheap and the test surface is the milestone

BinTable.Owner and RuleInstanceTable.Building are six sites each. CreateBin and FindBin are four sites in src/ and 44 in tests/. The repair is small and the churn is where the assertions are.

F19 · before a line was written

Three tasks were one task, and an ADR had already said so

Reading minimal.toml against World.Fit showed tasks 2, 3 and half of 4 could not be separated — because a Rule's inputs and outputs are BinRefs. adr/0141 refused that exact split in its Rejected section; the decomposition split what the record governing it had declined to split.

Two latent defects, and one of them was already on main

F36

A saved table outside World._tables is not hashed

UnpremisedTable shipped with two saved columns and was not in the array the State Hash walks. 2,074 tests passed without noticing. The corpus's own guarantee — declaring a field is what allocates it, so the hash cannot have a coverage hole — is true per column, and says nothing about whether the table is walked. Found because a hash did not move when it should have.

F38

The census reserves room per family with a hand-kept constant, and it was wrong twice

A counter written past its family's region lands in the next family and reads back as that family's data. ZoneCounters was wrong since task 4, committed and pushed — so tenancies ended and placement considered printed the identical four numbers. ⚠ Instrument output only; no State Hash moved.

F39 — naming the shape was worth more than either fix

Both are a declaration and a hand-maintained number that must agree, with nothing checking that they do — [Table] against _tables, and a counter enum against its slot count. In both, the declaration is the source of truth and the number is a copy. A copy of a fact is the copy that drifts. Two tests now close the two instances; the class is not closed — nothing enumerates the remaining hand-kept counts in this codebase.

The closing task found the milestone's own mechanism was invisible

F43. Task 4 made a failing tenant end its tenancy and leave the premises standing. Task 10 went to show it, and there was nothing to point at. On all twelve shipped Ruleset files the premises' upkeep starves while the tenants' restock keeps their larders full — so the premises fail and the tenants never do.

Every test of the mechanism built its Ruleset by hand, which is why nothing noticed. A mechanism exercised only by hand-built fixtures is a mechanism with no world in it — and the difference is invisible from inside the suite.

F44 — the long run's stated obligation was the wrong collection

The plan said the long run's new obligation was the unpremised pool. It cannot be: nothing creates a Business, so that pool is empty in every world and the no-unbounded-growth rule has nothing to ask of it. The collection milestone 25 actually introduced is the tenant's Rule Instances and Bins, whose lifetime task 2 tied to the tenancy — a per-tenancy allocation cycle this project did not previously have. The plan named the visible new table and missed the one that was a change in lifetime rather than a new type.

F45 — and it holds. Over 131,072 Ticks at 4,000 Citizens with roughly 1,900 tenancies ending per sample window, the bin and rule_instance slot counts are flat at 3,362 and 1,440. A flat high-water mark under continuous churn is rows being recycled — which is the claim the rule actually makes, and something a live count alone could not have shown.

ACT 4

What is still open

Six things the milestone filed rather than fixed, and one it handed forward

The project's rule is that a finding about code a task does not own gets routed on the day, before being worked around. These are what milestone 25 routed.

F35

Nothing records why a tenancy ended

The condemnation trail names a Lot, so an entry there would be a demolition record for a Building still standing. The channel that carries why is this Household unhoused belongs to adr/0130 and ships with the gate. Filed rather than improvised — a second trail keyed on the Household would be the wrong thing to have to delete.

F20

The two halves of adr/0141 cannot both be whole

That ADR puts a tenant's Bins on the [[business]] table, and a Household has no kind table and never will — so the marker went on the [[building]] bin entry, where the ceiling is declared. Milestone 27 has to say which table names a Business's Bins. Filed as a correction owed, because nothing in group A can test the answer.

F26

MigrateBins does not reach a tenant's Bins

It rotates one Building's list. A Resource leaving the Ruleset leaves a Household holding a Bin naming an id the incoming file does not declare — inert rather than crashing, because the rebuild guards the read. Unbuilt, not refused.

F32

A walk that mutates its own list bit twice in one milestone

Once in task 2, once in task 4, both caught by reading rather than by a test. It is now a recognisable shape in this codebase. A WalkRemovable helper is unbuilt, not refused.

F39

The declaration-versus-hand-kept-count class is not closed

Two instances found, two tests written. Nothing enumerates the remaining hand-kept counts, so the third one will be found the same way the first two were.

F6

derived = "occupancy" has lost its only caller

The one declared Readout in the project, left declared with nothing reading it — because consume stopped being one Rule applied occupancy times and became one Rule per Household.

What capitalises a Business

A hash-bearing number owed a named ratifier — a machine, a world and a quantity. It travelled with tasks 6–9 to milestone 27. ⚠ Milestone 26 gained a blocker rather than losing one: it was blocked on 25 and is now blocked on 25 and 27, which is the honest reading of what was always true.

Where it left the project

The payer is nameable. A local money term has an actor to resolve to, a tenant's Bins leave with the tenant, and a starving family loses its lease instead of pulling the building down on its neighbours. Nothing yet creates the actor — that is milestone 27, and it is the risk this one was capped short of.
