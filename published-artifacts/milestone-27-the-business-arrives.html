Milestone 27 — the Business arrives

Milestone 25 made the payer nameable. This one makes one exist. The city now creates a Business two ways, funds it out of a founder's balance, employs through it, and lets a Rule read its books — and the task order had to move four times to get there.

ACT 1

The risk it retired

That the Business is a table and not an actor the city can create

This is milestone 25's original risk, inherited unretired when that milestone was capped at group A. It came in four quarters, and the milestone is closed because all four are answered.

Creates

Two channels, both shipped

A premises kind may declare its trade and construction instantiates it (adr/0148); a Household founds one on its own means (adr/0145). Both land unpremised, so nothing auto-tenants.

Funds

Out of the founder's balance

Founding is a transfer, not an issuance — it moves money out of a Household and conserves the supply. A Household founds on its own means and never on the city's need, because a trigger reading a shortage is the RCI meter whatever it is called.

Employs

jobs moved to the trade

The Workplace stops being a Building. A Citizen still stores nothing about their day — and the property that defends gets stronger: changing employer now changes their day with no write, which was literally untrue before.

Reads

A Policy sweeps a population

sweeps = "business" is accepted, and a Readout now declares the set of entities it can be read against rather than a single scope (adr/0149).

G1 — the risk cell stated three numbers and all three had drifted

It said the Business had three columns (six, since milestone 25's tasks 1 and 5), that jobs sat on the dwelling kind in all twelve shipped Rulesets (thirteen, since evicted.toml landed the day before), and that CreateBusiness had twelve test call sites (seventeen, across seven files). The risk itself was untouched. A risk restated in counts is a risk that goes stale without becoming wrong — and it was the second time in two days that a milestone's own risk cell was the thing that was out of date.

ACT 2

What planning found

The order moved four times, and each move was a different kind of mistake

This milestone inherited its specification from plans/0040 and wrote only its order. That turned out to be the hard part. Decomposition changed the order, and that is the document's result — then the order moved three more times, twice by correction.

AS SPECIFIED

6 → 7 → 8 → 9

The order the tasks were written in when milestone 25 was capped. Nobody had asked what each one needs.

MOVE 1 · G5

6 → 9 → 8 → 7

Run it as specified and task 7 empties the city of jobs. jobs = 8 sits on the dwelling kind in every shipped Ruleset as an admitted stand-in; move it to an employer nothing creates and employment goes to zero. ⚠ Measured rather than argued — minimal.toml patched to jobs = 0 as a proxy gave 53 failures of 2,094, of which four were a confound and two were self-editing tests. The remaining ~47 are the city.

MOVE 2 · G21

6 → 8 → 7 / 9

Task 9 was opened and put back down. Its stated reason for going second was that it is exercisable by fixture — which is the reasoning plans/0040 F43 had refuted the day before: a mechanism exercised only by hand-built fixtures is a mechanism with no world in it. Task 9's case was stronger than F43's, not weaker: it would have been reachable by no Ruleset at all.

MOVE 3 · G30

6 → 8 → 7 → 9

Two unrelated things on one day. The one open decision blocking task 7 had been settled all along by an ADR the decision never cited — and adr/0146 made task 7 a prerequisite of task 8's founder half, because the founder is recorded by the employment link and an unpremised Business has no Building to point at.

MOVE 4 · G32

6 → 8ᵃ → placement → 7 → 8ᵇ → 9 → 10

Task 7 still could not go next, and the reason is arithmetic rather than judgement: nothing tenants a Business. Founding fills a pool; employment needs premises, and nothing carried a Business from the first to the second. ⚠ PlacementEngine's own source comment said this milestone owed the pass — and none of its four tasks named it. The pass shipped as adr/0147.

G30 — the shape of a phantom blocker, which is worth more than the answer

An open decision asked whether jobs moves to the business kind or the Business row, and concluded it was forced by what adr/0026 means by employer — an ADR that never says. adr/0141 does say, in a table row headed Declares whose two columns are both kind namespaces, and the build was already reading it that way in a doc comment.

The tell is that the decision named the ADR it could not get an answer from. A question typed arguable and pinned to one silent source looks blocked with no further work, because the next step it implies is a sitting rather than a search. Typing a question does not discharge the duty to look for its answer — and the cheapest check, grepping the term across docs/adr/, was never run. It cost the milestone an ordering.

And one about how a blocker is read

G13

The capitalisation band was called a block, and the ADR refuses that reading in its opening paragraph

adr/0052 opens: "This does not require ratifying a number before choosing it… The rule governs the record, not the timing." What task 8 owed was a ratifier named on the day the band was written, not a settled number. Citing a record is not applying it — which is that ADR's own stated failure mode, at its line 39. So the ADR predicted the way it would be misread, and a plan citing it by name then misread it that way.

G24

The source question was never a number, and the ledger had it typed as one for a day

A prior sitting concluded the question was not what creates a Business but what capitalises one — "and that is a number." Opening task 8 found there is no first line of code in that reading: a band with nothing to draw it is not a source. ⚠ A question that changes type has not necessarily been answered; it may only have been narrowed — and settling it properly tripled the ledger row, into a founding band, an arrival band and a founding rate.

What delayed finding it was a header

founded.toml opened with a long paragraph predicting that its money supply would drain out through the Hinterland on purpose. The supply was draining; the paragraph explained it; nothing looked further. Naming an expected symptom is how you stop noticing the unexpected one that looks identical. Both of that header's claims were wrong, and it has been rewritten to say so at length.

ACT 4

What is still open

One gap nobody owns, one silent failure class, and a bound that will reopen

G22

Nothing can give a Business a Rule, and no task in the milestone owned it

Run the arming pass for a Business and a bakery inherits the Rules of the families living upstairs — which is adr/0141's own argument inverted. A Business's Bins and Rules must come from its [[business]] kind, and task 6 deliberately gave that kind nothing but a name. ⚠ The tenancy enum is Premises | Occupant and cannot express which tenant. Unassigned by tasks 6, 7, 8 and 9 alike.

G31

There is no unknown-key check in the loader, so every typo in every Ruleset is silent

Every table reads the keys it wants by name and nothing ever enumerates a table's actual keys. occupent = 3, revisit_tics = 1024: all load clean, all do nothing, none says a word. ⚠ It is the exact failure class this loader has 150-odd refusals to prevent, arriving underneath all of them. Not fixed — the reason is scope. One named refusal shipped for the single key a designer will actually type, and that is not a substitute for the check and must not be recorded as one.

G44

The shop count is bounded by the source exhausting, and no sink has ever fired

7,165 premisings and 0 give-ups over 131,072 Ticks at 2,000 Citizens — placement re-premises a pooled Business long before the bound expires. What bounds the count is that founding is a means test that runs out of means: each one moves money out of a Household, so each makes the next less likely. ⚠ The long-run test therefore asserts a deceleration rather than a ceiling. A bound resting on a source drying up reopens the day anything refills it — milestone 11's gate, milestone 26's revenue.

G40

A Readout can now be wrong about how many entities it means

A single scope made over-permission unreachable — a Readout could be wrong about which entity, never about how many. Declaring a readable set gives that up to say something true. A Readout carelessly added to two sets is a Rule reading a quantity that means a different thing on each, and no test in this corpus can catch it. Recorded in the ADR's revisit triggers rather than guarded, because the guard would be a claim about what a scalar means.

G29

No audit names a saved column with no simulation writer

CitizenTable.Employment was saved — therefore hashed — with nothing in src/ writing or reading it, so the golden baseline was folding a fixture's arithmetic. adr/0146 gave the column its first meaning, which is defining it rather than extending it. ⚠ The general defect is unowned: this is the mirror of a derived-but-never-rebuilt column, and no test asks the second question. Found by grepping for something else.

wage

Two ADRs disagree about the wage and neither says so

adr/0141 puts the wage in the trade's Declares row; adr/0026's whole mechanism is that the wage moves, each Business adjusting its own by its own fill rate. A kind declaration is Ruleset data — identical across every Business of that kind — so a number that adjusts per-Business cannot be one. The reading that makes both true: the trade declares the posted anchor and the current wage is a row column. ⚠ That is a reading, not a decision, and it is milestone 15's to settle.

§D1

Every number these worlds produce ratifies nothing

A shop has no revenue — Scope.Pool still throws — so a levy on one is a levy on capital. founded.toml's two founding numbers and the levy's percentage are all in the unratified ledger. Do not quote a money figure out of a run of either file as anything about the economy.

Where it leaves the project

The economic actor exists in a city rather than in a fixture, and there is no longer a shipped world without one in it — which is the opposite answer to the one milestone 25's closing task got, one milestone later. Next is milestone 26, the purchase: Scope.Pool stops throwing, and a Rule can finally spend money between two actors that both exist.
