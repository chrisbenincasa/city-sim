Borough · engine note · 24 Aug 2026

The rule engine, and what it does not decide

A tour of the two Rule families, the authoring surface a designer actually gets, and an honest count of how much of the city is prescribed versus produced.

How much does it control Less than it looks. The engine is a deliberately underpowered interpreter — no predicates on Bin Rules, no expression language, no authored prices.
Where is the emergence Structurally protected but mostly unbuilt. The refusals are real and enforced; the mechanisms they clear room for are largely not shipped yet.
Are the rulesets fixtures All fifteen of them. Every shipped file's own header says it models no city. There is no game ruleset in the repository.

01 · The shape of it

Two execution models, sorted by one question

The whole engine turns on a single sorting test, stated in docs/02 §4: subscribe when waiting on a specific named thing; poll when sweeping a population. Everything else follows.

A Bin Rule is an atomic transformation over Bins — integer stores on a Building or its Occupant. It lives on the Event Wheel, fires on a rate, and when it fails it does not retry on a timer: it goes to sleep on the exact Bin that stopped it and waits for a mutator to wake it. A Sweep Rule fires on a plain interval, walks a whole population, tests real state with a predicate, and acts on whoever qualifies. It never sleeps and never subscribes.

Which family a mechanism belongs to is fixed at design time and is never a performance decision, because the two differ in observable behaviour — in how fast an arriving input propagates, in who wins a contested resource, and in the sentence Evidence prints when they fail.

02 · The authoring surface

Everything a designer is allowed to say, and it is a short list

Here is the entire content of a Bin Rule as it appears in a shipped file. There is nothing hidden behind it — this is the whole grammar.

[[rule]]
name    = "consume"
kind    = "dwelling"
rate    = 32                                               # reschedule interval, in Ticks
apply   = { min = 1, max = 1 }                             # greed band — or `derived`, never both
inputs  = [ { scope = "local", resource = "sundries", amount = 4 } ]
outputs = []
on_fail = "no_repairs"                                     # optional: chain to another Rule

Four scopes exist, and the third column of that table is the load-bearing one. A scope does not answer where do I look for this Bin — it answers whose is it.

scopeMeaningWhoseBuilt?
localBins on the subject running the Rule — its Building, or its Occupantmine, freelive
poolthe District Pool — a market, requiring road connectivitysomebody else's — a tradethrows on reach
globalcity-wide Bins — the treasurythe city'slive
mapMap Layer cells under the footprint, write-onlynobody'spollution only
The single most important refusal

A term crossing an ownership boundary is not a transformation, it is a trade — the Good moves one way and money moves the other at the prevailing price. There is no Ruleset syntax for that payment. The price is emergent, the quantity is already the term's amount, and the counterparty is implied by the scope. Nothing is left for a designer to write, which is what keeps amount a fixed integer permanently.

The four things the engine will not let a designer do

These are enforced, not advisory. Each one is a place where a designer could have prescribed an outcome and is prevented from doing so.

RefusalWhat it would have boughtWhy it is refused
No predicates on a Bin Rule "only produces if staffed", "only in dense Buildings" Nothing to subscribe to, and it would be the only way in the design for a Building to do nothing without saying why. Every such case dissolves into a physical input that has not been modelled — staffing becomes a labour Bin filled by arriving commutes, so a failed commute causes a legible production failure.
No expression language arbitrary formulae over state amount is a fixed integer for ever. Proportionality comes from an apply count — a Rule runs 1..n times — and nothing else. "15% of gross income" is one unit of money applied gross × 15 / 100 times. Integer, deterministic, no parser.
Two declared Readouts Rules reading any scalar in the world The readable set is Occupancy and Balance. A Readout is admitted only when a Rule reads it, and declaring one is a decision to let every future Rule act on it. Everything else is display-only — the inspectable surface is far larger than the actable one.
No global scalars at all an RCI demand meter, tax tolerance, a difficulty modifier All three are named in CONTEXT.md's banned-terms list. There is no demand scalar — the Unplaced Pool is the demand signal. Tolerance is a Household comparing the city against a Hinterland with the same utility function everyone uses.

03 · Where it runs

The Tick is the determinism contract, and each family has its slot

The contention story is where prescription would be easiest and is most carefully avoided. When a Bin is short, its wait list drains from the head, by requirement, derived at the moment of the drain — a Bin holding six wakes exactly the one bakery that needs six, and a Rule that fires goes to the back of the queue. The result is that a district degrades as a gradient: under half supply every bakery bakes half as often, rather than half the bakeries running normally while the rest starve. Nobody wrote "degrade gracefully"; it falls out of queueing plus indivisible servings.

05 · The honest read

The guardrails are real. The emergence is mostly still promised.

5distinct Rule names authored across all fifteen shipped files
2Building kinds in the entire corpus — dwelling and port
1on_fail chain that actually exists, one link deep
0of the five design Goods named by any Ruleset

That strip is the finding. Your worry — that designers end up prescribing the city through the rule engine — is not the risk this project currently carries, and the reason is uncomfortable rather than reassuring: almost nothing has been authored yet, because almost nothing can be.

The pool scope throws when a Rule reaches it. Every one of the five design Goods (Produce, Food, Timber, Materials, Consumer Goods) is produced by one Building and consumed by another, which crosses an ownership boundary, which is pool. So the shipped files name sundries — deliberately a word for goods too unimportant to name — and a Ruleset that could run today and also name a real Good would be claiming a chain it cannot express.

The real risk, restated

It is not over-prescription. It is that the refusals are load-bearing on mechanisms that do not exist yet. "There is no demand scalar because the Unplaced Pool is the demand signal" is only true while the Pool has pressure in it — and until crowded.toml was written, no world this project could generate had a non-empty Pool at all, because the city generator houses everybody. Eleven tests failed on their fixture before one failed on its assertion.

The same shape recurs: the land-value producer shipped correct and unobservable because no file emitted pollution. The District watershed was untestable because every generatable world had one centre. The Business existed in fixtures for four milestones before anything created one.

The project has a name for this and a rule against leaning on it: an unbuilt mechanism is not a design constraint. The simulation does not do X almost always means nobody has built X, and the answer to given X does not exist, should Y compensate? is build X. That rule is what has kept compensating knobs out of the Ruleset so far. It is doing more work than the engine's own refusals are.

What the cost picture says about future authoring

There is a live constraint on how much a designer will ever be able to write. One Rule evaluation measures 82.84 ns as a synthetic unit and 552 ns in a real world — 2.8× higher, attributed to terms, scatter and population. Carried forward at the project's unratified assumption of 450 Rule Instances per 1,000 Citizens, a million-Citizen city spends roughly 10.5 ms a Tick on the Rule engine alone, against a 15.6 ms budget on one core of a 2020 six-core desktop. Two-thirds of the frame, before roads, layers, or growth.

Read that as a design fact, not a performance one: the engine cannot afford an elaborate authored chain per Building, so the expressive poverty above is not only principled, it is priced.

06 · The rulesets

All fifteen are fixtures. There is no game ruleset in this repository.

This answers your third question directly, and the answer is stronger than you probably expected. Every shipped file carries a header explaining what it exists to demonstrate and what it must not be read as, and most of them open with some version of the same sentence: it models no city, and it is not the beginning of one.

Thirteen of the fifteen are minimal.toml with a single controlled change — usually one number, sometimes one table, once two deletions. That is deliberate: it makes each file an instrument rather than a second city, so a difference in behaviour is attributable to the one thing that moved.

The discipline worth keeping

Each file's header states not only what it shows but what it must not be quoted as. evicted.toml: a dwelling whose larder nothing fills is an absence of a mechanism rather than a hardship, so the numbers this file produces ratify nothing. crowded.toml: a demonstration that chose its own rate would be showing the demonstration. This is the guardrail against a fixture's tuning leaking into the design as a settled number — and it is why the corpus insists you quote the sentence, never the digits.

The open question this leaves is not about the engine. It is that nobody has yet written a Ruleset that tries to be a city, so the claim that a small authoring surface produces a rich one is, at this point, an argument rather than a demonstration. The first file that names real Goods and a real production chain is the one that tests it.

07 · Kinds and the enumeration question

A kind is a flat list with no inheritance — so the design spends its effort on not needing many

There is no template, no base kind, no parameterisation. A [[building]] declares its name and seven optional numbers, and that is the entire vocabulary for saying what a Building is:

[[building]]
name         = "dwelling"
bins         = [ { resource = "sundries", capacity = 48, owner = "occupant" },
                 { resource = "repairs",  capacity = 4 } ]
occupants    = 4        # how many Households it holds
jobs         = 8        # how many Citizens it employs — a separate number
parking      = 8
condemn_after = 4       # consecutive Rule failures before it is condemned
business     = "shop"   # which trade may tenant it

Three hard ceilings fall out of the storage layer, and they tell you the intended scale better than any prose does. A Building's kind is a byte, so the loader refuses a file declaring more than 254 Building kinds and more than 254 trades. A Lot's permission set is a 16-bit field, so there are at most 16 zones — ever.

And a Rule names exactly one kind. So does a Zone Rule. Two kinds that behave alike need two copies of every Rule block; there is no way to write same as dwelling, but forty Occupants.

So the answer to "is the designer expected to enumerate everything?"

Yes — but the design works hard to make the list short, by refusing to let three axes that would normally multiply the catalogue become kind axes at all. The discipline is stated in docs/deferred.md: adding a category is a design decision rather than a content addition.

The factorisation that does the most work

A shopfront hosts a bakery, then a barber, and the building did not change. Under one kind table that sentence is inexpressible — bakery would be a property of the walls, so a tenant leaving would have to demolish the shop to stop being a bakery. Two namespaces turn a product into a sum.

Axes deliberately kept off the kind

AxisWhat the genre doesWhat this design does instead
Quality / wealth / level Cities: Skylines authors growable prefabs into mutually exclusive asset pools with a level 1–5 ladder. A high-density cell cannot spawn a house, ever. refused Called placing buildings by proxy. Quality is not a kind axis at all — it comes out of the choice model and the price system, which already produce it.
Density A painted band that commands what appears. a permission set Density is capacity, not quality — a high-density slum and a high-density tower are the same band. A band expresses itself as which kinds a Lot permits, and 16 bits is the whole namespace.
Occupancy and jobs Separate building types per size. two numbers occupants and jobs are keys on the kind. They are separate numbers on purpose — a workplace houses nobody and employs a hundred; a mixed-use Building does both.
Infrastructure variants Road, rail and port as three subsystems. one abstraction Three kinds differing only in throughput ceiling, price, favoured Goods, and the mode mask their Arc carries. The variation is numbers, not mechanism.
Appearance The asset library is the type system. not in the Ruleset Nothing in a [[building]] names a mesh. The simulation has no visual vocabulary, so how one kind maps to many looks is a renderer question nobody has had to answer yet.

What still enumerates, honestly

Two costs survive the factorisation, and both are real.

  • Density bands are kinds. Stack — an apartment block — is defined as a kind with a larger declared occupancy. So low / medium / high residential is three [[building]] blocks, and because a Rule names exactly one kind, it is also three copies of every Rule those dwellings run. Nothing shares.
  • Services have no variant axis at all. How many kinds of school, clinic, plant or station there should be — and, more importantly, what distinguishes them — has been raised and deferred every time it has come up.
Open, unowned, and quoted verbatim from the ledger

"On what axis do Service variants differ? … Capacity alone makes variants a ladder the player graduates up, which is exactly the error adr/0025 refused for Density. The axis should be settled before any variant is authored, even though the catalogue itself is Ruleset content."

That is plans/0002 item 28, sitting in the no document covers it bucket. It is the precise question you just asked, it has been open since session five, and no ADR owns it.

So the current position is coherent but incomplete. The design has refused the enumeration ladder — no levels, no wealth tiers, no asset pools — and has factored out the one product it identified. What it has not done is name the axis on which two schools legitimately differ, and until it does, every additional kind is a hand-written block with its own duplicated Rules and a byte of a 254-wide namespace.

08 · Worked examples

The same entity, configured six different ways, and what each configuration costs

Five of the six below are lifted from files in rulesets/ and will load as written. The third is constructed — nothing in the repository authors density bands yet — and is marked where it appears, because a fixture's numbers must not be quoted as the design's.

A

One [[building]], at both ends of its range

A kind declaration can be one key or eight. Both of these are real, and they are the same table.

portbordered.toml
[[building]]
name = "port"
arrivals_per_day = 12
One key, and its presence is what declares the kind an Outside Connection. It houses nobody, employs nobody, holds no Bin and runs no Rule — a Household housed in the gate is not a Household that came through it.
dwellingminimal.toml
[[building]]
name = "dwelling"
bins = [
  { resource = "sundries", capacity = 48,
    owner = "occupant" },
  { resource = "repairs",  capacity = 4 },
]
condemn_after = 4
occupants     = 4
business      = "shop"
parking       = 8
Two Bins on opposite sides of the tenancy line, a condemnation threshold, an occupancy ceiling, a permitted trade, and parking supply.
B

The same Bin, owned two ways

This is the one line in minimal.toml that decides whether a Rule belongs to the premises or to the tenant. Nobody put the test there deliberately — it was found later, and it became adr/0141.

the lardergoes with the baker
{ resource = "sundries", capacity = 48,
  owner = "occupant" }

[[rule]]
name  = "consume"
kind  = "dwelling"
rate  = 32
inputs = [ { scope = "local",
             resource = "sundries",
             amount = 4 } ]
The premises declare the capacity; the tenant holds the level. local here means the tenant's. The Rule Instance and the Bin are both created when the Household moves in and freed when it moves out — an unhoused Household has no kind to read a ceiling from.
the roofstays with the walls
{ resource = "repairs", capacity = 4 }
// owner defaults to premises

name  = "upkeep"
kind  = "dwelling"
rate  = 16

             resource = "repairs",

Fires once whatever the occupancy, and its failure is what condemn_after demolishes the premises on. Same table, same syntax, different actor — and a Rule that addressed both sides would be refused at load, because that would be a trade.

D

One shopfront, three trades — the factorisation paying off

Compare the shape of this against C. The premises declare capacity once; the trades are a separate namespace, and a tenancy pairs them at runtime.

the premisesdeclared once

               capacity = 48,
               owner = "occupant" } ]
occupants = 1

The walls hold what fits in the shop. What is in it belongs to whoever is trading there this year.
the tradestenanted.toml, verbatim
[[business]]
name = "bakery"
jobs = 8

[[business]]

Ids live in their own namespace, so a trade and a premises kind may share a name and stay different declarations. Two trades rather than one, because one cannot demonstrate that ids are assigned in declaration order within a namespace.
What the split buys, in one sentence

The barber replaces the bakery and the building did not change. Under a single table, bakery would be a property of the walls, so a tenant leaving would have to demolish the shop to stop being a bakery — and condemnation, which takes the longest failure pressure across a Building's whole Rule list, would let one starving tenant condemn the other's shop.

E

One Rule, three apply counts — same throughput, three different cities

This is the configuration axis most likely to be mistaken for a tuning knob. It is not: the three produce different observable behaviour, and choosing between them is a modelling decision fixed at design time.

greedyworks through its stock
[[rule]]
name = "bake"
rate = 32
apply = { min = 1, max = 4 }
inputs = [ { scope = "local",
   resource = "flour", amount = 4 } ]
Applies as many times as the inputs allow, and fails if it cannot reach min. Drains its Bin to the floor, so it crosses the supplied/short boundary more often — and chain-walking is paid on entry into shortage. A designer choosing greed is choosing a visible behaviour and paying for it.
fixedowes a quantum
[[rule]]
name = "upkeep"
rate = 16
apply = { min = 1, max = 1 }
inputs = [ { scope = "local",
   resource = "repairs", amount = 4 } ]
Performs a defined quantum or fails. Not a second mechanism — min = max is the degenerate band. Infrastructure upkeep draws cost ÷ effective life per Day and must never draw more because the treasury happens to be full.
derivedconsults a Readout
[[rule]]
name = "consume"
rate = 32
apply = { derived = "occupancy",
         percent = 100 }
inputs = [ { scope = "local",
   resource = "sundries", amount = 4 } ]
Runs once per unit of the Readout. A derived count of zero is a success, not a failure — an empty Building re-arms normally and explains itself. Declaring a band and a derived count on one Rule is refused: their failure semantics collide.

Greed handles what is consumed; a derived count handles what is consulted. Flour is spent, so stock decides n. Fertility is never consumed and greed cannot see it, which is why both specifications have to exist. The percentage form is also the whole reason there is no expression language — "15% of gross income" is one unit of money applied gross × 15 / 100 times.

F

The strongest example in the repository of configuration changing what a city is. Same roads, same kind, same population. Two Rules deleted.

And the caveat that travels with it

The file's own header: a dwelling whose larder nothing fills is an absence of a mechanism rather than a hardship, so the numbers this file produces ratify nothing. The two runs are the same city with the failure moved from the premises to the tenant — which is the point, and is why it is a two-Rule diff rather than a new world.

What the six add up to

Configuration in this engine varies along four axes and no others: which Bins a kind has and who owns them, how many Occupants and jobs it declares, how a Rule's apply count is specified, and which Rules exist at all. Everything a conventional city-builder would express as a building type — wealth, level, quality, desirability — is downstream of those four rather than declared beside them.

The cost of that austerity is example C. The payoff is examples D and F.
