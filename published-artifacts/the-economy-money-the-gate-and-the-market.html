The economy — money, the gate, and a market that cannot trade yet

Three milestones that gave the city a conserved balance sheet, a door for people and money to arrive through, and a District with a price in it. Two of the three retired their risk in full. The third was capped, and its risk was rewritten to match what it actually did.

What this page is, and what owns the facts on it

Why the cap counts as the scheme working

"Capping a milestone re-opens the question of what it was for" — and this was the first time that had been asked of a partially-shipped row. What stopped task 7 was not the throw: a purchase needs a payer and there was not one. Money lives on a Business, a Rule Instance named a Building, and a Building holds a list of Businesses. ⚠ That blocker had been predicted eight milestones early, in a table's own doc comment — where no mechanical check could ever see it, because every corpus check is document-to-document.

ACT 2

What planning found

A dependency graph that was blind by construction, and a question seven decisions had not asked

10 · F1 and F6

The roadmap's dependency graph was systematically blind to bounding conditions

"A dependency graph derived from what a mechanism needs in order to exist can miss what it needs in order to function" — and the tell is that every edge in it reads needs X, which shipped. Every missing edge turned out to be an anchor edge. The repair forced a re-ordering that admits exactly one arrangement: 9 land value, 10 money, 11 the Hinterland, 12 the District Pool, 13 the price surface, 14 the Provider List.

12 · decision 10

Ordering the work found a question seven prior decisions had not

The Pool was counterparty on both sides of a trade, and the two sides happen at different Ticks — so where is the money in between? Nothing in the plan, and nothing in the three ADRs the milestone was built on, asked it. ⚠ It was sized as a head-of-task-7 question and that sizing was measured false by trying it, the same day: it did not block one task, it reopened the ADR the milestone rested on.

12 · the survey

A milestone whose risk is a single throw reads as a milestone with a single obstacle

The survey found three preconditions no document listed as blockers, two of them structural — there was no District table and no District id at all. "A scope that resolves through an entity is blocked on that entity existing, and the throw says nothing about it." The largest single piece of unscoped work in the milestone appeared in no inventory, no blocker list and no risk statement.

12 · the sitting

Three mechanisms were parked on an assumption their authors never checked

Upkeep, freight, and the volume comparison — each parked at milestone 12 by a different document, against one blocker its author happened to be holding. All three were unplaced in one day, by asking what the milestone actually ships.

And the decision that closed by dissolving

Decision 10 escalated to a sitting of its own and closed the same day — void as posed. "It was never a question about money. A District owns nothing… the Pool was never a party to the trade — the seller is. The question was the shape of a missing counterparty." Stock stays in the selling Building's own Bin; the District's row is the price, the wake target and the reachable sellers. ⚠ The prior ADR was amended, not superseded: it had decided reach, and custody was a reading nobody had argued for.

ACT 3

What shipped

A closed circuit, then two doors cut into it, then a market with nothing to trade

Milestone 10 — and the counterfactual is the interesting half

The acceptance run is one 100,000-Tick run of rulesets/taxed.toml, read every 2,048 Ticks — the Policy interval. The money sum is 174127 at every one of the 48 readings. Not a band, not a tolerance, not a drift bound: one value, forty-eight times.

Then the same file, the same length, with the rebate's draw pinned to a constant. Its flat 100 comes out of a treasury holding one Day's levy, so it funds 172 of 360 Households and then stops — leaving 172 at the ceiling and the other 188 holding exactly zero, for ever. And every conservation reading is identical to the healthy run's.

The headline is not about conservation

A conserved economy tells you nothing about who holds it. That is the argument for the money supply and the treasury being two reported figures rather than one — and it is why the long run asserts a distribution alongside the sum.

Milestone 11 — the generator could not reach its own map edge

A gate stands on a map edge. Before any code was written, the generator was measured against that requirement on minimal.toml at 1,000 Citizens: 160 paved Tiles out of 16,384, 124 Lots — six on the west edge, fifteen on the south, none on a corner, 103 inland. Reaching the map's full extent would have taken on the order of 2.6 million Lots.

An edge a generator cannot reach is a market nothing can arrive from. The repair paves the lattice to the boundary, taking that world from 36 nodes and 61 Segments to 263,169 and 535,817, laid in 150 ms.

Which is the design working rather than failing

A far gate is made usable by a dwelling beside it, not by a faster road. A pure-arterial run of that distance is 43 minutes, and no route of that shape is pure. The map is sized by how many commutes fit across it, so a gate past the budget is the sizing rule doing its job.

Milestone 12 — and the market is inert on every world that exists

Six tasks shipped: a world with two centres joined by a Street corridor, a Building-density field, a watershed that derives Districts from it, re-evaluation with hysteresis, a Pool Bin per Good, and a price bounded by the cheapest import across every declared Hinterland.

And then the milestone's own summary of it: the price is built, correct and inert on every world that exists — for the third time. Nothing writes a consumption bucket while Scope.Pool throws, so every rate is zero, every recompute reads no trades, and every price sits at its ceiling from Tick 0 to the end of the run.

ACT 4

What is still open

A ledger of unratified numbers, two defects only a long run could find, and one document that disagrees with another

The project distinguishes an unset number — a gap, which blocks work and cannot corrupt it — from one that is in use and unratified, which is the actual debt. The ledger's own motto: an unratified number is more dangerous than an open question.

§D1

Every [districts] and [market] key is in use and unratified, and the world that would ratify them cannot

The density field in the only world with Districts in it is flat, so the two concentrations never touch at a saddle, no boundary is ever contested, and the prominence threshold is never consulted — the same assertion at 1, 50 and 100 gives two Districts every time. The hysteresis band has to be exercised by a hand-built fixture. The named ratifier is milestone 15's agglomeration.

→ 26

Tasks 7–10 kept as written, because they are the specification milestone 26 inherits

The purchase itself; the Provider kind and its market Ruleset; something to look at; and the long acceptance run. ⚠ Two of those carry blockers that were named inside a task entry and therefore invisible to the ledger that owns what is next — the Provider kind's three content decisions, and the fact that no shipped Ruleset produces a world where a Building genuinely runs out of money.

11 · F31

A duration bound produces a size bound only after a settling time of its own order

The Pool's ceiling is inflow × gives_up_after_days. On the two-Day file it settled at 1,458 by Day 4, as predicted. On the 120-Day file it reached 11,653 at Day 244 and was still climbing ~50 a Day — while the bound worked exactly as designed: mean wait 30 Days, longest 122, four members over the bound out of 11,646. The value did not move; the row's refutation condition was corrected in place.

11 · F30

A defect eleven milestones old, reachable only once something else shipped

A parking release had one caller, and it was not the one that frees the row. Found by the acceptance run at Tick 65,664, reading 234 occupied spaces against 233 holders. Why nothing caught it sooner: until the give-up bound landed, the function that would have exposed it had no production caller at all.

11 · F26

A cost figure was filed, then withdrawn the same day — and the withdrawal is the lesson

A gated world costs 38.7 ms a Tick was wrong: 38.4 ms of it was a debug guard that folds the whole world twice a Tick. With the guard off, that world runs at 0.51 ms a Tick against minimal.toml's 0.16. ⚠ The withdrawn figure was the only one in the tick-budget file not taken with the guard off, and it was filed beside figures that were, without saying so — Cause 5 occurring inside the document that exists to catch it.

12 · F38

The only shipped world with Districts in it had never been run for two Days

It ran clean at 2,048 Ticks and panicked by 4,096. The cause was the check, not the code — a derived extent describes the city as of the last evaluation, so a Cell demolished at Tick 1,152 keeps its membership until Tick 2,048, and the end-of-run check was asserting that the cadence had never run. ⚠ The finding behind the finding is that nothing in the suite runs that file.

⚠ docs

Two documents disagree about what blocks milestone 26

The milestone 12 plan's cap banner says 26 is blocked on milestone 25. The roadmap row says blocked on 25 and 27, and notes it has now been wrong in both directions. The plan was not updated when 25 was capped. The gate board is the authority on gate status, and the roadmap row says so itself — but the stale copy is still sitting in the plan.

And one thing stated so nobody discovers it later

Import is priced but not embodied. Between milestone 12 and freight, a distant gate costs nothing at all — there is no haulage term, so every gate is equidistant and a city simply buys at the cheapest. ⚠ Do not read a milestone-12-era city as evidence that gate placement is free.
