## Why these five, and where the boundary lies

Sacramento supplies a missing postwar American contrast. South Bend and Amersfoort preserve useful earlier plan evidence. Copenhagen adds range/garden and construction-system alternatives; the Mediterranean lane tests roof/party-wall/mixed-use differences against the northern-European bias. Five assembled studies are enough to test the selection idea without superficial national coverage. Southern Europe has weaker whole-building evidence here, so its street remains a more provisional alternative.

Neither Sacramento's preservation survey nor protected Tingbjerg establishes ordinary prevalence. They are useful component/context evidence whose selection bias remains visible. The single celebrated Grand Parc transformation is retained only as a retrofit mechanism. No celebrated project determines the default building mix.

## Questions requiring judgment

Player: which street relationships feel like the desired city; how colourful; how much variation within one neighbourhood; whether coherent named contexts are useful beyond the fictional start.

Content/simulation: tenancy-to-dwelling mapping; residential versus workplace kinds on large bodies; coverage of porches, balconies, courts and services; future-only selection versus reskinning; any actual family/age frequencies.

Research/engineering: measured ordinary European commercial plans; documented local floor heights; room/egress/daylight plans; vehicle swept paths; a specific low-pitch covering specification. None is replaced by a convenient invented “regional standard”.

## Regional association versus the underlying cause

This is an interpretive classification, not a statistical causal study. The sources establish examples and construction relationships; the factor attribution guides modelling.

| Visible feature | More useful underlying variable | Regional interpretation to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Broad eaves and carport | Era, structure, exposure and vehicle/site arrangement | Every American house has a porch or garage |
| Low felt gable versus flat roof | Complete roof system and construction period | Northern climate mandates steep tile roofs; DK1–3 provide counterexamples |
| Long range and repeated stair doors | Circulation, structural repetition and housing programme | All European apartments are one corridor block |
| Narrow party frontage | Plot subdivision, party conditions and development history | Dutch paint creates a Dutch house on any width |
| Recessed opening, shade or terrace | Solar exposure, privacy, envelope and maintenance | Every southern-European building uses one warm-colour/terracotta package |
| Large workshop bay or blank service wall | Use, structural span, loading and equipment | Employment buildings are residential bodies with fewer windows |
| Front parking versus street edge | Development era, movement and redevelopment policy | A single difference within Sacramento proves a US/Europe divide |

Relevant counterexamples and limits are in [CA1–4](atlas/index.html#CA1), [NL1](atlas/index.html#NL1), [DK1–3](atlas/index.html#DK1), [ES1](atlas/index.html#ES1) and [C1](atlas/index.html#C1).
