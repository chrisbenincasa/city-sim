# Mixing at building, street and neighbourhood scale

**Provisional choice: one temperate fictional context with discrete families.** The mixture is demonstrated in [monochrome](drawings/fictional.svg) and then [materials](drawings/fictional-materials.svg). These are proposed designs, not a fictional place presented as surveyed fact.

Within one building, mix compatible finish systems through a legible joint: masonry base and panel upper walls, replaced shopfront, added shading, repaired parapet. Keep the load path and water shedding explicit. Roof swaps, courtyard cuts, parking undercuts and gallery additions are new designs rather than independent style parameters.

Along the fictional street, the attached row meets the apartment through a four-metre ground gap. The apartment meets corner commerce through another four-metre gap. The corner moves to the street line. The rear workplace and workshop connect to a continuous six-metre service lane. W1 also reserves a four-metre rear apron connected to the side street for its receiving door; turning remains untested. These distances are authored reservations, not local standards. The apartment, homes and commercial bodies do not share one stretched roof.

Between neighbourhoods, a detached tract, open-range estate and compact perimeter block can coexist as different development histories. They need different ground/layout contracts. The temperate start does not transplant every Sacramento carport or Mediterranean exposed gallery into a cold/wet setting; climate, exposure and maintenance remain eligibility checks.

| Control | Meaningful scope | Standing |
|---|---|---|
| Context preset | Eligible family catalogue + climate/material choices + compatible layout | Proposed; five comparisons supplied |
| Influence slider | Relative selection weights among already eligible, genuinely distinct designs | Possible later; no UI recommended yet |
| Material variation | Palette and compatible finish changes | Useful independently; material sheet follows geometry |
| Era/renovation | Original family plus later intervention | Separate controls; no sourced frequency distribution |
| Density/layout | Plot subdivision, party walls, courts and access networks | Content/simulation choice, not a skin |

If a slider is later tried, filter designs by footprint, storeys, allowed use, access, edges and ground first; then interpolate selection weights. Do not interpolate vertices, roof pitches, unit counts or stair plans. If no candidate fits, report the gap or supply an explicitly authored valid fallback. A midpoint does not promise half the land, residents or floor area from each influence.

A useful test would compare a fixed-seed set at three settings using different whole designs and ask the reviewer to identify access/family differences without colour or labels. The current package performs the noninteractive architectural comparison; it does not claim a player test, statistical distribution or working eligibility implementation.

Counterexamples: American flat-roof apartments; Dutch pitched and flat rows; Danish low gables and flat-roof systems; Spanish ventilated and solid flat-roof assemblies; commercial portals used across regions.
