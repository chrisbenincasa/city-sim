# Three model briefs and reusable construction rules

**Everything specified for M1–M3 is PROPOSED.** These are dimensioned research models, not reconstructions, certified building designs, approved gameplay capacities or a production asset kit. Documentary controls are identified separately. All linear model values are metres unless marked otherwise. Use real scale; do not shrink the mesh inside simulation-owned ground to make it look domestic.

## Rules shared by the models

| Height | T01 documents two storeys; A01 reports eaves/ridge separately | Explicit floor levels; never interpret roof storeys as full floors or randomly jitter overall height |
| Facade bays | T01 has two per house; M02's real reference is a nine-bay composition | Bays follow rooms, stairs and shop structure. Whole-row count grows through repeated dwelling units |
| Structural spans | No measured joist/truss sections obtained | Reserve load paths; use party walls/internal supports for houses and a frame for M3. Do not present these choices as structural calculations |
| Roof | Brick/slate ranges and glazed links at Evans; different flat-roof and parapet examples in atlas | Pitch, covering, drainage and abutments form a package. Don't combine arbitrary pitch, tiles and roof shape |
| Openings | Plans establish access relationships; most opening dimensions remain missing | Fix a small proposed schedule below. Vary joinery/condition within it rather than window sizes independently |
| Material module | Modern standard brick 215 ×102.5 ×65 mm; Marley plain tile 265 ×165 mm [S17](https://www.wienerberger.co.uk/products/brick/standard-format-bricks.html), [S18](https://www.marley.co.uk/-/media/066d3695d9a94cb7a8019b1db5175f1f.pdf?rev=27071efb996241869e2faffc511005fe) | Proposed 10 mm mortar gives 225 ×75 mm face module. Clip at openings; never stretch to fill a wall |

A 500 ×250 mm slate is a catalogue option, not a measured historic tile. For the initial slate surface, **propose**100 mm headlap and 200 mm double-lap gauge. The actual applicable headlap depends on exposure, rafter length and pitch; this catalogue's flattened columns were not reliable enough for pitch-specific transcription. [S19, PDF pp 20–21](https://www.sigaslate.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/documents/SIGA-Slate-Brochure.pdf)

For low-slope roofs, model a deck, waterproof covering, edge/upstand, outlets and overflow. M3 chooses a visible 1:40 fall as a prototype parameter. Bauder's general page gives the older 1:40 design/1:80 finished convention, while its indexed 2025 update says design falls depend on analysis. **This brief does not assert a current regulatory minimum.** [S20](https://www.bauder.co.uk/technical-centre/standards-and-knowledge/flat-roof-design-considerations), [S21; full page requires login](https://www.bauderace.co.uk/technical-centre/standards-and-knowledge-9835597dbd573da8ab6ee78cd0e3b81f/bs-6229-2025-key-changes)

## M1 — five-house terrace

**Risk tested:** whether individual house plans and entrances make a long range read as homes rather than one oversized house. Controls: T01's repeated two-bay houses, T02's inspected narrow-house plan and Goldsmith's contemporary street rhythm. Historical style and modern plan dimensions are being combined deliberately, not represented as a measured Saltaire reconstruction.

- One unit 4.8 wide ×9.6 deep; five units produce a 24 ×9.6 m main range. Two storeys at ground 0, first 3.0, eaves 6.0. External wall reservation 0.30; party-wall reservation 0.25. These thicknesses are proposed.
- Two facade bays per unit, centres 1.2 and 3.6 from the left party line. Front entrance in the left bay:0.95 wide ×2.10 high; window opening in the other bay 1.05 ×1.50. First-floor windows use both bay centres; sills 0.80 above floor. Reveal 0.15; sill projects 0.06; lintel height 0.225. Windows and doors have actual recesses at close range.
- Front hall/stair lies beside the party wall; front room faces street, rear room faces yard. Reserve 1.0 m clear stair width and a 1.2 m landing; roof/floor supports must avoid the opening. Rear door 0.90 ×2.10. This establishes topology, not fully furnished floor plans.
- Ridge runs along the street. Roof crosses 9.6 m wall span at 35°: rise `4.8 × tan(35°) = 3.361 m`; ridge 9.361 m above datum. Eaves project 0.25. Continue the same pitch over the eaves; the outer eave drops about 0.175 m. Party walls terminate the roof/attic compartments; timber floor span is principally between party walls, with a purlin/support arrangement for the roof. Do not render a 9.6 m unsupported rafter beam by assumption.
- Gutters on front/rear eaves; proposed 0.125 m gutter width and 0.075 m downpipes at selected party lines. Ridges, verges and lead apron/step flashing are geometry at close range. Two proposed chimney groups at party walls,0.75 ×0.45 m, projecting 0.8 m above adjacent roof surface; no random chimney in a valley.
- Prototype plot 4.8 ×23 m:1.2 m front threshold strip,9.6 m main building,12.2 m rear ground. One selected house gets a 3 ×4 m rear lean-to, eaves 2.4 m and high abutment 3.2 m; use sheet roofing for this low pitch. It has its own external door and drains toward the yard. The remaining rear ground stays open. A rear lane 1.8 m wide lies beyond the plots.
- Warm brick, slate roof and muted joinery first; stone is a separately reviewed local variant. All five units share the structural pattern; entrance paint, patched mortar and a limited extension history provide variation. Model abandoned copies with repairs, shutter/board state and gutter failure, not different house scale.

**Exclusions:** no side windows through party walls; no repeated deep lean-tos closing every yard; no independently random floor heights; no inferred residents. Five physical dwellings are a proposed architectural count. Mapping them to one or several game Buildings/Lots remains a content decision.

## M2 — corner shop with homes above

**Risk tested:** whether a corner reads through entrances, floor hierarchy and an attached service side. Controls: M01's shop attachment, M02's mixed-use range and M03's corner treatment. There is **no measured historic shop width in this bundle**; the envelope is provisional.

- Main envelope 9.6 ×12 m; ground floor 3.6 m, upper floors 3.0 m each; eaves 9.6 m. Primary street along the 9.6 m edge; secondary street along the 12 m return. Keep both street elevations composed; use quiet rear/service elevations.
- Primary front: shop display zone 6.0 m wide broken into three 2.0 m structural bays, with 0.30 m piers and recessed glazing; proposed display glass height 2.4 m above 0.45 m stallriser. Remaining frontage accommodates a 1.0 ×2.2 m independent residential entrance and stair bay. Put shop entrance on the return close to the corner,1.2 ×2.2 m, with 0.6 m recess under the upper envelope. Do not remove a triangular corner from simulated ground without accounting for it.
- Upper facades: four 2.4 m bays on primary front, five on return; selected 1.05 ×1.5 m openings, with blind/offset stair bay where the plan calls for it. Ground shop divisions need not match every upper window, but the supporting piers/header must explain the difference. Fascia height 0.45 m fits below the upper floor; keep residential entrance legible outside the shop sign.
- Reserve a 2.4 ×5.0 m stair/lobby strip behind the residential entrance. Proposed one dwelling per upper floor, two total; retain separate kitchen/bathroom service stacks at the rear. Actual households and shop tenancy allocation are unresolved.
- Main roof ridge along the 12 m depth;9.6 m cross-span at 35°, rise 3.361 m; eaves 0.25. This roof is higher than M1's ridge. Where the terrace meets its side, resolve the lower roof against the taller wall with stepped flashing; do not intersect two complete roofs invisibly.
- Add a 3.6 ×4.8 m rear service/workshop range with 3.0 m walls, low sheet roof and explicit gutter. Proposed loading door 1.8 ×2.4 m opens to service ground, not through the home stair. Reference W01/W02 explains the relationship, not these dimensions. This attachment adds covered ground; its eventual simulation representation must be resolved.

**Exclusions:** no residential access through the shop; no shop glazing on every rear bay; no decorative truncated-pyramid cap; no projecting sign obscuring the home entrance; no unreviewed assertion that the ordinary game Building contains exactly one shop and two Households.

## M3 — a small apartment block on the existing G003 ground

**Risk tested:** whether a 24 ×16 m, three-storey component can look believable without reducing its 1,152 m² counted floor or substituting small houses. Documentary controls: A01's narrow apartment ranges, A02's explicit common access/flat roof, and A03's 75 m² apartment and shared-lobby precedent. This is a later infill building within the provisional context.

- Preserve the 24 ×16 m footprint and 10.5 m wall height. Floors at 0,3.5,7.0; roof deck nominally 10.5. Proposed reinforced-concrete frame reservation 6 ×8 m, with brick infill/outer leaf; beam and slab depths require follow-on design. No fictitious traditional timber span across the full 16 m.
- Central corridor 24 ×2 m along the long axis, with two 4 ×5 m stair reservations at its ends, overlapping the corridor. Add a 2 ×7 m central front lobby from the street edge to the corridor. This reserves 86 m² per floor in plan after overlaps; it leaves 298 m² per floor for four apartment envelopes, average 74.5 m² each **before walls/shafts**. This arithmetic is proposed layout allocation, not net usable area or measured capacity.
- Proposed 12 apartments over three floors, four per floor. Reserve eight principal 3 m facade bays; group pairs within units. Use 1.20 ×1.50 m windows plus smaller service openings according to plan. Central front door 1.2 ×2.2 m, canopy 2.4 ×1.2 m, address/mail panel and separately expressed stair windows. Two side stair doors connect to 2.4 m side paths and then the street/rear garden. Do not attach party-wall neighbours over those paths or windows.
- Flat-roof assembly: parapet 0.45 m above deck,0.20 m wide, with coping and internal waterproof upstand. Proposed transverse falls 1:40 over 8 m runs give 0.20 m level difference; direct to a longitudinal collection line, divided to two outlets with visible overflow routes. Keep slopes and drainage on the roof plan, not just in a texture. Reserve a 2 ×2 m roof-access headhouse over a stair, height 2.4 m, set back from the street. No giant hip or pitched cap is required.
- Warm brick, slate-grey coping/roof and painted or metal joinery; material joints and slab/brick relationships differ from M1. Later infill should not imitate every historic cornice. Compare occupied/abandoned states with identical silhouette first; use local blocked drains, patched membranes and boarded glazing only as proposed visual condition states.

**Exclusions:** no twelve illuminated unit patterns presented as actual simulated occupants. The current sample allows two shared tenancies, so live mapping is unresolved. No claims of regulatory accessibility, fire/egress compliance or proven structural feasibility. These reservations are sufficient for the exterior research model; they are not engineering certification.

## The next small street and review sequence

Create a standalone **60 ×60 m research scene**. Use scene bounds x=−6…54 and y=−32…28, in metres: primary building line at y=0; plots extend toward positive y; primary pavement 2.4 m and carriageway 6 m extend toward negative y. Five M1 homes occupy x=0…24; M2 occupies x=24…33.6, with a perpendicular side street beyond its return. Across the primary street, place M3 with a 2.4 m side access on each side. Keep the opposite building line a proposed 14 m away. This is a scene parameter inspired by S05's street dimension, not a claim that the same endpoints were measured there. Locate one low workshop at the rear of M2 and a continuous rear route beyond M1 yards.

Review in this order:

1. **Untextured massing:** matched orthographic plan, front, side and rear; then street eye height 1.75 m. Confirm roof intersections, side access, gutters and attachment ground. Compare original G003 beside M3 at identical scale.
2. **Moving camera:** fixed saved camera paths at street, neighbourhood and city distances. Ask whether terraces, shop entrance and shared apartment access remain legible. These distances and performance costs are hypotheses until observed.
3. **Materials:** hold models/light/camera constant; compare brick/slate at actual modules before any art-scale enlargement. Reuse textures independently of mesh proportions.
4. **Condition and overlays:** compare occupied/abandoned, day/night and overlays without changing massing. Verify roof edges, door thresholds and major attachments remain legible; show simulation occupancy only after its mapping is resolved.

Instance repeated bays, windows and terrace units with fixed pivots at party-wall/ground intersections. Keep silhouette geometry (roof, chimney, canopy), close construction detail (reveals, flashings), material detail (courses, joints) and condition masks separate. No draw-call, memory or frame-time claim is established by this research. Follow the drive skill and build Debug when the subsequent Godot prototype is actually run.
